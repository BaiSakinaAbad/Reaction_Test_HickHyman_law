THEORY PAGE

Hick's Law, also known as the Hick-Hyman Law, is a psychological principle stating that the more choices a person is presented with, the longer it will take them to make a decision. Mathematically, this is often expressed as:

The relationship between the number of choices and the decision time is logarithmic, meaning that the decision time increases with the number of options, but the rate of increase slows down as more options are added.

In simpler terms, doubling the number of choices doesn't double the decision time. However, having too many options can lead to cognitive overload, making the decision-making process slower and potentially frustrating for the user.

For GUI design, Hick's Law underscores the importance of minimizing user choices to reduce decision time and cognitive load; this involves limiting menu items and navigation options, prioritizing on-screen actions, keeping forms concise, implementing effective search and filtering for large datasets, simplifying complex processes, and employing progressive disclosure. Ultimately, applying Hick's Law helps designers create intuitive and efficient interfaces that prevent user frustration by offering sufficient options without overwhelming them.
